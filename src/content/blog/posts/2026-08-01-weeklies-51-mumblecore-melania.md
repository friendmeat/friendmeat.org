### Derrick McCormack

The title "Sh\*thole Oprey" made me check out [this episode](https://podbay.fm/p/wake-island-broadcast/e/1784019600) of the WAKE ISLAND podcast, and in the course of the conversation I realized I've heard of Derrick McCormack before and I've had [_Castle Faggot_](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9781635901375/castle-faggot/) bookmarked for years.

#### _Dark Rides_

A quaint compendium of unsettling Canadian countryside vignettes. Derrick gets his balls shocked for conversion therapy. Derrick's friend lights herself on fire with homemade fireworks they'd been selling on the road. Derrick knocks a coworker's lights out and presumably rapes him. They all have the same dry literary tone that relies on unfolding details and juxtopositions for tension.

#### _The Show That Smells_

_Smells_ couldn't be more of a 180 from _Dark Rides_. It's a fever dream of a bad 80s horror B-Movie: foppish fashionista vampires descend with a dark agenda upon a circus, and in putting their plot in motion, they make a Faustian bargain with American country singer Jimmie Rodgers. The aforementioned _Shithole Oprey_ is an art book building on this concept, presumably creating a Shithole Oprey Cinematic Universe.

#### D.C.F
The "Deconstructed Concert Film" is a doomed genre. _The Moment_ suffers all the same issues as another D.C.F, _The Nowhere Inn_ (2020). They even use the same trope: a fan in a meet-and-greet gets hysterical, cries: "your music saved my life!"

These scenes are the problem in a nutshell. The talent is confronted with twin pressures-- the histrionics of the fan and the managerial need to keep the line moving. They are repulsed by, empathetic to, and pity the fan. The gulf between fan and star is impassable, and the point: the fan is not invested in the person of the star, but in the commodity. Fanaticism is financial leverage. 

As Jaime Brooks has discussed[^1], the confessional turn in pop music is equal parts democratization via consumer tech, cultural zeitgeist, and industrial exploitation. On the industrial side of things, the century-old interlocking mechanism of promoter release and tabloid scoop keep The Spectacle going, irrespective of the individual star. The talent-fanatic duplex flow is independent of either, and the hit parade will march straight over each of them.

The star senses her fungibility, and wants simultaneously to expose the industrial relation and individuate herself from it.

The D.C.F. is an attempt at individuation through authenticity. But no matter how mundane your real-life problems are, making a movie about it is not a method accessible to normal people. There is only mythologization. And _The Moment_ and _The Nowhere Inn_ are aware this. That's why they lean into an amalgam of the authentic and the fabricated, and even the surreal in _Nowhere Inn_ (as facile and ineffective it is). But that creates another problem: what are we watching for?

The point is that a concert film is a status marker. It marks an artist's graduation into a certain network of movers and shakers. The D.C.F. lets those inclined mark that notch in their belt without having to feel like they 'sold out'. The DCF probably ends of costing way more than just doing a regular concert film, anyway.

### KOTH Season 3 is some creepypasta shit
Why is King of the Hill season 3 suffused with so much darkness. I accidentally skipped over it at first, and the adjacent seasons don't have a fraction of its misery. It opens with violent, fiery death of Luanne's long-term boyfriend. The cast spends the rambling episode ruminating on death and grieving.

The episode where Bobby experiences his first breakup not only gives us [that classic frame of abject misery](https://i.kym-cdn.com/entries/icons/mobile/000/037/567/coverbobyascend.jpg), but also a performance from the voice actor that's simultaneously gut-wrenching and cringe-inducing from the second hand embarrassment. It's more emotion than you'll see in most films.

The cold open of the Christmas episode is just Bill sobbing while everyone stares silently. It's some creepy pasta shit.
