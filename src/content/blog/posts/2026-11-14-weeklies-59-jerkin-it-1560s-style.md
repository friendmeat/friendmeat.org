## __something i hated__
### parking racket / justice system synergy
My city is one of many that has attempted to cling to life by selling off its parking enforcement to a [vampiric for-profit middleman](https://www.spplus.com/municipal/). One of their rackets is a street-cleaning schedule which produces reliable revenue like as follows:

On a semi-monthly schedule, the streets are cleaned on alternating days: one side on the first day, the other side on the second day. Cars parked on the side being cleaned are ticketed. This is ostensibly to encourage residents to make way for the municipal workers offering a public good.

However, street parking is also divided into zones. Each zone has a different street cleaning schedule. Within a zone, there are not enough spaces if every car were to be parked on only one side of the street. Therefore, the obvious solution would be to park in a different zone during your zone's street cleaning day-- except 1) this would create knock-on congestion issues in the rest of the city as street-cleaning refugees flee to other zones and 2) the city-issued parking permits are only good within your zone. Therefore, if you park in another zone to avoid a ticket, you also get ticketed.

To make matters worse, are no garages or lots in my neighborhood, and the ones downtown-- also managed by the same third-party enforcement company that issues the tickets-- are among the most expensive in the state.

This leaves us in a Hobbesian *State of Warre* where neighbor vies against neighbor in a vicious battle for parking, with the losers are left to the salivating maw of the for-profit parking enforcers. The ticket is $30, with an extra $8 "processing fee" when you pay online. If you're late on payment, you receive a warning and incur a $20 penalty.

Apparently I got a ticket in early October. Either I forgot to pay it, or lost the ticket, or the ticket was removed from my windshield by unknown means-- all likely scenarios. The parking enforcement company issued me a delinquent notice... 

...except it must have been lost in the shuffle at the post office, because despite being issued weeks ago it only appeared this week, *alongside the court summons it threatens me with*[^1].

So between the ticket and court fees, I had to pay over $100. Which isn't even bad compared to the time I got a ticket every day for a week because my permit had expired without my knowledge (the permit renewal schedule is its own disaster). That ended up being something like $500.

And in case you were worried, rest easy-- the court's payment processor still got $10 for processing my guilty plea.
